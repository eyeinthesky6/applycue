# ApplyCue CV Tailoring Policy

## Purpose

A CV is persuasive marketing, not a verbatim archive, an affidavit, or a classroom exercise. ApplyCue helps the user recover and present relevant experience for a specific employer. The objective is a polished, readable application that earns a serious conversation; an ATS keyword score is not the objective.

The supplied CV is not assumed to contain everything the user has done. Missing from the CV means "not yet captured", not "did not happen".

## Baseline

Preserve the exact supplied CV before editing. Keep a larger evidence-rich base CV; role CVs can be shorter. A baseline search must be recorded before major base-CV rewrites so the user can see the product's effect.

## Enrichment

With permission, the agent may read selected local projects, GitHub, LinkedIn, websites, Linktree, portfolios, blogs, posts, certificates, case studies, and writing samples. It must present material additions, conflicts, chronology issues, and authorship questions for confirmation before adding them to the base CV.

For a specific role, the agent may also ask targeted memory-recovery questions based on the employer's likely concerns. Examples include government or regulator engagement, a forgotten product launch, sales ownership, budget or team scope, architectural trade-offs, evaluation work, difficult stakeholder decisions, or qualitative outcomes for which no exact metric survives. Do not ask a generic interview questionnaire or require documentary proof. A clear user recollection and confirmation is valid candidate evidence; save it once in the approved user evidence layer before using it in generated documents.

When an exact number is unavailable, use a confirmed qualitative outcome or scope statement instead of inventing precision. For example, "reduced turnaround time" is acceptable when the user confirms the result but cannot recover the percentage.

## Employer success brief

Before writing any role CV, the agent creates a small internal brief from the full JD and bounded company research:

- what the employer explicitly asks for;
- the likely outcomes expected in the first 6-12 months;
- the team, customers, partners, or executives this person must influence;
- the role-family lens and the three strongest signals the first page must communicate;
- likely recruiter or hiring-manager doubts and the evidence that can answer them;
- company language, mission, operating context, and current priorities that materially affect the application.

Keep employer statements, sourced company context, agent inference, and unknowns separate. The agent may infer what success probably looks like, but it must not present an inference as a quoted employer requirement.

## Role-family positioning

Do not use one technical-CV formula for every role. Choose a primary role family, and a secondary family only for a genuine hybrid:

| Role family | Evidence to lead with |
| --- | --- |
| Technical | architecture, delivery, tools, reliability, scale, and trade-offs |
| Product | problem discovery, product choices, what was not built, adoption, and business results |
| Sales / GTM | customers, pipeline or revenue, conversion, partnerships, market creation, and commercial ownership |
| Operations / programme / public impact | ecosystem coordination, delivery systems, government or partner work, evaluation, policy, responsible scale, and measurable impact |
| Strategy / consulting | diagnosis, choices, executive influence, implementation, and resulting change |
| Founder / operator | ambiguity, resource allocation, products launched, commercial learning, and team leadership |

The summary, first-page proof, bullet order, vocabulary, and selected projects should express the chosen family. They may change substantially between applications while the underlying facts remain stable.

## Claim signals

- **Sourced:** clearly present in the baseline or approved evidence.
- **Reframed:** same underlying fact expressed with JD language or clearer outcomes.
- **New/unconfirmed:** material fact, measure, ownership, title, date, credential, or capability that needs confirmation.

These labels guide the conversation; they are not automatic moral rejection. Once the user confirms a claim, it may be used. If the user does not confirm it, omit or soften it.

The labels are internal review aids. Do not clutter the finished CV with provenance labels and do not force the user to approve line-by-line wording. The agent has freedom to write positioning, emphasis, connective language, and a clear professional point of view from the confirmed facts.

Never invent employer, role, date, degree, credential, metric, legal status, authorization, product ownership, team size, or achievement. Never turn “used a tool/project” into “built or authored it” without confirmation.

## Standard and high-stakes applications

Every application receives the employer success brief, role-family positioning, recruiter-risk review, and targeted fact recovery needed to produce a credible CV.

The user may mark a role **high stakes** in chat or on the dashboard. A job link supplied directly by the user defaults to high stakes unless they say it is standard; agent/scanner discoveries default to standard. The selected priority is durable in the existing dashboard-feedback ledger. V1 will add a repeatable saved campaign record. A high-stakes role adds:

- deeper research into the company, team, strategy, culture, current priorities, and selection context;
- a pattern review of public profiles or biographies of comparable successful people at the same or adjacent level, when lawfully available, focusing on experience signals rather than personal or protected traits;
- a positioning brief covering the three promises to lead with, material gaps, honest bridge language, and evidence still worth recovering from the user;
- a role-specific CV, cover letter or form narrative, and relevant portfolio/case-study recommendations;
- optional draft changes to LinkedIn, website, GitHub, portfolio, or public bio when those surfaces materially affect this application.

Social or portfolio recommendations are drafts. ApplyCue never publishes or changes a public profile without the user's separate approval. Most applications should not trigger profile changes; reserve them for genuinely coveted roles where the upside justifies the effort.

## Agent ownership

The external agent reads the full JD, builds the employer success brief, asks only material evidence questions, and writes the role CV. Text similarity code may report keywords but may not select/reject claims or decide fit. Preserve the user's voice unless they approve a new style.

## Required artifacts

For an application, retain:

- tailored Markdown source;
- tailored HTML source;
- ATS-friendly PDF;
- ATS-friendly DOCX;
- the role report/JD reference.

Before upload, open/parse the selected file and confirm candidate, company/role context, and expected sections. Broken, empty, wrong-role, or missing files are hard blockers.

## User review

Before drafting, ask the user about missing evidence only when it could materially improve or change the application. After drafting, show a concise positioning/change summary and unresolved facts; do not make the user approve every punctuation edit. Final application approval covers the named CV/application package only.

## Outcome boundary

ApplyCue can optimize the quality, clarity, relevance, and integrity of the application. It cannot promise an interview. Product evaluation should measure shortlist precision, user effort, preparation time, application completion, and eventually interview conversion across a useful batch—not an isolated manual form fill or an ATS score.
