# Recruiter-Side Application Heuristics

Use these rules for candidate-facing documents and answers: PDF summaries,
bullets, cover letters, form answers, LinkedIn messages, recruiter scripts, and
interview prep. They do not apply to internal evaluation reports unless a mode
explicitly asks for analysis.

## Employer Success Brief

Before drafting, make a small internal brief from the complete JD plus bounded
company context. For a standard application, use the JD, an official company or
team source, and one current priority/source when it materially changes the
message. Record:

- what the employer says it needs;
- the likely first 6-12 month outcomes;
- who the role must influence;
- the primary role family;
- the three signals the first page must communicate;
- likely doubts, evidence, and honest gaps;
- which statements are sourced, inferred, or unknown.

For a user-marked high-stakes role, also inspect a small number of relevant
public profiles or biographies of successful people at the same or adjacent
level, when available. Extract recurring experience signals, scope, language,
and proof patterns. Do not copy another person's identity, wording, career, or
protected traits. Ask the user targeted questions when a useful signal may be
part of their experience but is absent from the current CV.

The old CV is not a complete inventory. A user-confirmed recollection is valid
evidence even when no document or metric survives. Use qualitative scope or
outcomes when confirmed; never manufacture precise numbers.

## Role-Family Lens

Choose one primary family before selecting proof:

- **Technical:** architecture, delivery, reliability, scale, tools, trade-offs.
- **Product:** problem discovery, choices, prioritization, adoption, business outcomes.
- **Sales / GTM:** customers, pipeline/revenue, conversion, partnerships, commercial ownership.
- **Operations / programme / public impact:** delivery systems, ecosystem partners, evaluation, policy, responsible scale, impact.
- **Strategy / consulting:** diagnosis, choices, executive influence, implementation, change.
- **Founder / operator:** ambiguity, resource allocation, launches, learning, team leadership.

For a hybrid, choose one secondary family. Do not create a CV that tries to be
all families at once.

## Recruiter-Side Risk Map

Before generating a CV, cover letter, form answer, recruiter script, or
interview prep, create a small internal risk map:

| Potential doubt | Evidence from CV/report | Candidate-facing fix |
|-----------------|-------------------------|----------------------|
| Can they do this stack? | Matching tools, systems, projects | Put the exact stack in truthful context |
| Are they senior enough? | Ownership, scope, tradeoffs, mentoring | Lead with senior behaviors, not tenure alone |
| Is the domain relevant? | Similar users, workflows, scale, constraints | Translate adjacent proof into the JD's language |
| Do they understand our actual outcome? | Employer success brief, relevant decisions and results | Lead with the result this role is expected to create |
| Is there a logistics blocker? | Location, comp, work-auth, availability | Answer only where the form/recruiter asks |
| Is the application generic? | Weak or broad bullets | Rewrite around this role's concrete problems |

Use the map to reduce review risk. Do not print it unless the mode output
explicitly asks for analysis. Never invent evidence to close a doubt.

## Six-Second Clarity Gate

For every CV/PDF and cover letter, the top third must make the target fit
impossible to miss:

- target role/archetype
- strongest matching stack or domain
- one production or business outcome
- location/remote fit only when appropriate for that document
- portfolio/case-study link when available and relevant

If a recruiter must infer fit from scattered bullets, rewrite the summary and
first experience bullets.

## Business-Value Bullets

Prefer:

`Action + system/scope + tool/approach + outcome + proof`

Good patterns:

- `Resolved [problem] in [system], improving [business/system effect].`
- `Built [capability] with [tools], enabling [user/team outcome].`
- `Migrated [old] to [new], reducing [risk/cost/latency/debt].`
- `Improved [metric] from [before] to [after] by [technical action].`
- `Chose [product/market direction] after [evidence/trade-off], leading to [adoption/business outcome].`
- `Opened [customer/partner/channel] by [commercial action], producing [revenue/conversion/market result].`
- `Aligned [government/partners/teams] around [programme/system], enabling [delivery/impact outcome].`

An exact metric is useful but not mandatory. A confirmed outcome, scope,
decision, or before/after description is better than false precision or dropping
important work simply because the number was forgotten.

Avoid weak starts when stronger ownership is true: "helped", "assisted",
"responsible for", "worked on", "participated in".

## ATS Reality Check

Optimize for parseability and human review, not "ATS hacks":

- exact JD keywords only in truthful context
- no hidden text
- no keyword stuffing
- no white-font tricks
- no decorative layouts that break parsing
- no skills or metrics not present in the user's sources of truth
